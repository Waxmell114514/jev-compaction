# Venue bar and research-to-harness influence for an LLM-agent context-management / harness-design paper (as of 2026-10-14)

Scope note: this covers (a) the accepted-paper landscape on agent context/memory/efficiency at top ML/NLP/SE venues 2025–2026 and their evaluation standards, (b) evidence of research actually influencing harnesses, (c) reviewer/statistical expectations and common weaknesses, (d) paper-shaped contributions extractable from `jevctx` and the experiments each would need, (e) venue fit and verified deadlines. Everything not marked with a source is inference. "Unverified" is marked explicitly.

---

## KQ1. Which 2025–2026 agent-efficiency / context-management papers were accepted at top venues, and what did their evals look like?

### Takeaway
The accepted set splits into two camps: (1) ML-venue papers (ICLR/ICML/ACL 2026, NeurIPS 2025) that mostly train or fine-tune a model to manage context (AgentFold, MemAgent, Context-Folding/ACL, ACE, ACON, MEM1) and evaluate on web/QA/AppWorld-style benchmarks; and (2) SE-venue/empirical papers (AgentDiet @ FSE 2026; Complexity Trap @ NeurIPS'25 DL4C workshop) that evaluate inference-time pruning on SWE-bench Verified with 200–500 instances, multiple models, dollar-cost reporting, and (in the best case) bootstrap CIs. The coding-agent pruning papers most similar to `jevctx` (SWE-Pruner, SWE-Pruner Pro, Squeez, Pichay) are still arXiv-only as of Oct 2026.

### Inferences
- Evaluation norm at the venues above for SWE-bench-style claims is: ≥200 instances (AgentDiet) and preferably the full 500 (Complexity Trap, SWE-Pruner); ≥2 frontier/strong-open models; dollar cost from the provider's actual price sheet (or provider-reported cost); 3 seeds where feasible; bootstrap CIs. The Complexity Trap's ±3–4-pt 95% CIs at n=500 show that a 21–24-instance single run cannot resolve any realistic solve-rate effect.
- ML main tracks (ICLR/ICML/ACL) have accepted context-management papers mainly when there is a trained component (SFT/RL) or a new optimization framework, and mostly on web/QA/AppWorld benchmarks; the pure inference-time, heuristic, coding-agent papers have landed at SE venues (FSE) or workshops (DL4C, Agents in the Wild), or remain on arXiv.
- The space of "tool-output pruning for coding agents" is now crowded (SWE-Pruner, Pro, Squeez, AgentDiet, Complexity Trap, Pichay). A new paper must position against at least SWE-Pruner, AgentDiet and observation masking as baselines.
- Cache-aware compaction and recoverable/lossless elision are discussed in blogs but no accepted paper found makes them the thesis; the only arXiv works touching recoverability are Pichay (fault-driven pinning, production-only eval) and the Sept-2026 "When Can Agents Forget" paper (reports cache-read tokens).

## KQ2. Evidence of research → harness influence; what made influential work influential?

### Takeaway
Direct, citable research-to-harness influence is rare. The strongest documented case is SWE-agent's "agent-computer interface" (NeurIPS 2024), whose vocabulary and design (structured file viewer, compact outputs) persists in SWE-agent and is used to describe OpenHands. Harness documentation almost never cites papers: OpenHands' SDK paper cites its own blog for the condenser's 2× cost claim; the April 2026 compaction survey of seven agents found only OpenHands citing any academic paper (its own SDK paper). Influence has flowed mostly from shipped artifacts + reproducible benchmark numbers, not from acceptance at a venue.

### Cited Findings
- SWE-agent (Yang, Jimenez, Wettig, Lieret, Yao, Narasimhan, Press), NeurIPS 2024: introduced the ACI concept; pass@1 12.5% SWE-bench, 87.7% HumanEvalFix; argues LM agents are a new category of end users needing purpose-built interfaces (sliding-window file viewer, filtered test output). — [NeurIPS 2024 proceedings](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/5a7c947568c1b1328ccc5230172e1e7c-Abstract-Conference.html); [GitHub](https://github.com/princeton-nlp/SWE-agent)
- Third-party evaluation paper describes OpenHands in ACI terms and SWE-agent "operating via an ACI", showing the term's diffusion. — [arXiv 2511.00872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.00872)
- OpenHands SDK paper (arXiv 2511.03690), §4.6: "LLMSummarizingCondenser (the default condenser) has been shown to reduce API costs by up to 2×", citing **Smith, C. (2025) All Hands AI blog**, not a paper; no other context-management or observation-masking work cited. — [arXiv 2511.03690 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.03690)
- That OpenHands blog (Calvin Smith, 9 Apr 2025): SWE-bench Verified subset (size unstated), condensation 54% vs baseline 53% solved, per-turn API cost ≤ half; cites no research papers. — [OpenHands blog](https://openhands.dev/blog/openhands-context-condensensation-for-more-efficient-ai-agents)
- OpenHands SDK condenser architecture: NoOp / LLMSummarizing / Pipeline condensers; defaults max_size=120 events, keep_first=4, target max_size//2; condensation emits a `Condensation` event with `forgotten_event_ids` onto an append-only log, view filtered at build time (history recoverable for replay); page cites no papers. — [docs.openhands.dev/sdk/arch/condenser](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/arch/condenser)
- The Complexity Trap explicitly targets "the industry trend toward pure LLM summarization" (OpenHands, Cursor) and reports that observation-masking's window must be retuned per scaffold (OpenHands). An `ObservationMaskingCondenser` class existed in the older `openhands.memory.condenser` package; whether the current SDK ships it is unconfirmed. — [arXiv 2508.21433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433); [old docs](https://docs.all-hands.dev/modules/python/openhands/memory/condenser)
- Compaction survey of 7 coding agents (Apr 2026): Codex CLI ~90% trigger, encrypted remote compaction blob for Codex models; Claude Code ~89%, five layers (no-LLM microcompact, clearing older tool outputs, full LLM summary, cross-session cache reuse, user compact instructions, thrash detection); Gemini CLI ~50%; OpenCode ~96–99% prunes old tool output first protecting last 40k tokens; Roo Code tags summarized segments with `condenseParent` UUID (non-destructive); OpenHands event-sourced suppression markers (full replay). **"Only OpenHands cites an academic paper for its architecture"; observation masking not mentioned for any agent; "no shipping agent is described as preserving the prompt cache through compaction."** — [codex.danielvaughan.com 2026-04-10](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/04/10/context-compaction-showdown-coding-agents/)
- Same blog (Jun 2026) on SWE-Pruner/Pichay/ContextBudget: "None of the three papers' methods appears in Codex CLI"; SWE-Pruner's skimmer "cannot run natively in Codex CLI". — [codex.danielvaughan.com 2026-06-17](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/06/17/context-pruning-research-swe-pruner-pichay-contextbudget-codex-cli-token-management-compaction/)

### Inferences
- What made SWE-agent influential: it shipped the harness (open-source, reproducible SWE-bench numbers at a time of few baselines), named a concept ("ACI") that practitioners could reuse, and the benchmark (SWE-bench) came from the same group. Vocabulary + runnable artifact + benchmark co-ownership drove adoption, not the NeurIPS acceptance per se.
- The Complexity Trap is the closest analogue to a `jevctx` paper and its practical effect appears to be agenda-setting (observation masking now widely discussed; tool-result clearing shipped by Anthropic, OpenCode and Claude Code prune old tool outputs first) but there is no citation trail proving causation; vendors may have converged independently.
- For a `jevctx` paper to influence harnesses, the deliverable that matters is a drop-in, scaffold-agnostic component (proxy or SDK condenser) with a reproducible SWE-bench result and a memorable concept name (e.g., "admission gating", "break-even compaction"), plus a PR into OpenHands/SWE-agent/mini-swe-agent or OpenCode. The OpenHands event-sourced condenser interface and Pichay's proxy design are the existing integration points.

- No primary-source evidence (commit, changelog, doc) that any harness feature was adopted *because of* a specific paper; all adoption evidence is circumstantial.
- Aider repo-map: searches returned only secondary comparison sites; no paper or academic citation found for it.
- Official Anthropic docs for context-editing thresholds were not fetched; numbers above are from secondary summaries.

## KQ3. Statistical standards: sample sizes and runs reviewers expect on SWE-bench Verified in 2026; is 21–24 instances / one run acceptable anywhere?

### Takeaway
No: 21–24 instances with one run per arm is below the bar for any main-track venue and for the better workshops. The ICLR-2026-workshop randomness study shows single-run pass@1 on SWE-bench Verified varies by 2.2–6.0 points run-to-run (SD >1.5 even at T=0), so 2–3-point effects are within noise even at n=500; accepted/credible papers use 200–500 instances, multiple models, ≥3 seeds where feasible, dollar costs from real price sheets, and bootstrap CIs. Cost-efficiency claims additionally face the HAL/"AI Agents That Matter"/Cost-of-Pass expectation that cost and accuracy are reported jointly (Pareto or cost-of-pass).

### Cited Findings
- 60,000 SWE-bench Verified trajectories (3 models × 2 scaffolds): single-run pass@1 varies 2.2–6.0 pp depending on run; SD >1.5 pp even at temperature 0; "reported improvements of 2–3 percentage points may reflect evaluation noise"; recommends multiple independent runs per task, **statistical power analysis** to size runs, and reporting pass@k (optimistic) and pass^k (pessimistic) alongside pass@1. — [arXiv 2602.07150](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.07150); [ICLR 2026 workshop listing](https://iclr.cc/virtual/2026/10016323)
- Related: 50 runs × 3 models on SWE-bench found more consistent models tend to be more accurate; single-run evals "may be misleading". — [arXiv 2603.25764](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.25764)
- Complexity Trap: n=500, paired bootstrap B=10,000, 95% CIs ±3.1–4.4 pp on solve rate, ±$0.03–0.26 on cost; marks significance with † — several solve-rate deltas of 5–10% relative were *not* significant. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21433v3)
- AgentDiet (FSE 2026): 200 randomly selected Verified instances, with a disjoint 100-instance set for analysis/hyperparameter tuning (held-out discipline). — [arXiv 2509.23586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23586)
- SWE-Pruner: full 500 Verified, temperature 0, 3 seeds "where applicable", dollar cost per instance. — [arXiv HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.16746v4)
- Harness-evolution study: 50 *stratified* Verified tasks per release used for a longitudinal study across 35 releases (acceptable because the design is many-releases × fixed-subset, not a single pairwise comparison). — [arXiv 2607.03691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03691)
- "AI Agents That Matter" (Kapoor, Stroebl, Siegel, Nadgir, Narayanan; TMLR 2025): agent evals should control for cost; simple baselines match complex agents on HumanEval at much lower cost; proposes accuracy–cost Pareto plots; warns of inadequate holdout sets. — [arXiv 2407.01502](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01502); [project page](https://agents.cs.princeton.edu/)
- HAL (ICLR 2026): "agents vary widely in costs, but evaluations rarely report these costs"; "scaffolds dramatically impact both accuracy and cost, yet comparisons across scaffolds are rare." — [arXiv 2510.11977](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11977)
- Agentic Benchmark Checklist (NeurIPS 2025 D&B): SWE-bench Verified has insufficient test cases; benchmark flaws can mis-estimate by up to 100% relative. — [arXiv 2507.02825](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02825v5)
- Practitioner guidance (secondary, MLflow): minimum 3 seeds per configuration, 5+ for publication-grade claims; report mean and SD. — [MLflow](https://mlflow.org/articles/tags/how-to-benchmark-ai-agents/)
- A deep-RL seed-count tutorial uses t-tests/bootstrap to compute required seeds for a given effect size. — [arXiv 1806.08295](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1806.08295)
- Informal ICLR-prep guidance notes reviewers "often flag 'No variance reported'"; a 2026 statistics playbook suggests ≥10 seeds per configuration (30 if the effect is small) and bootstrap 95% CIs (student guide, not venue policy). — [arXiv 2605.00428](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.00428); [claudskills iclr-reproducibility](https://claudskills.com/skills/iclr-reproducibility/SKILL.md)

### Inferences
- Minimum credible design for a `jevctx` SWE-bench Verified claim: full 500 instances (or ≥200 randomly sampled with a disjoint tuning set as AgentDiet did), ≥2 models including one frontier-class model priced at a real public price sheet (not a hypothetical sheet), ≥3 runs per arm (or power-analysis-justified), paired bootstrap CIs on both solve rate and cost, and an A/A arm reported as a noise floor (the existing 1.25× prompt-token A/A noise is a useful honesty signal but also tells reviewers the current n cannot detect sub-25% token effects).
- Token-count claims are less noisy than solve-rate claims; a paper can lead with token/cost reductions (with CIs) and present solve rate as a non-inferiority claim (CI of delta excludes a pre-registered harm margin), which is how Complexity Trap and AgentDiet frame it ("maintaining the same performance").
- Pricing on a hypothetical price sheet will be flagged; Complexity Trap was explicit even about Qwen pricing not distinguishing cache hits. Cache-aware claims need provider-reported cache-read/cache-write token counts, which Anthropic/OpenAI APIs expose.
- `mimo-v2.6-flash` as the only model is a weakness but not fatal if paired with one widely used model; SWE-Pruner Pro used MiMo-V2-Flash as one of two open-weight backbones.

### Gaps
- No official ICLR/NeurIPS 2026 reviewer-guide text about seed counts was retrieved; venue-level numeric thresholds are not published and expectations are inferred from accepted papers.
- The randomness paper's worked power-analysis numbers (runs needed per effect size) were not in the abstract; the full PDF should be consulted for exact recommendations.

## KQ4. Which `jevctx` claims could be the thesis, which has the clearest literature gap, and what is the cheapest convincing experiment?

### Takeaway
Of the four candidate theses, (ii) cache-aware compaction economics with a break-even rule and (iii) lossless/recoverable elision as a correctness property have the clearest gap: no accepted paper and no shipping harness (per the April 2026 survey) makes prompt-cache preservation a first-class design goal, and only Pichay (arXiv, production-only eval) treats recoverability seriously. (i) role-labelled judge gating competes directly with SWE-Pruner/Pro, Squeez and AgentDiet and would need to beat them head-to-head on the full 500. (iv) model-free supersession is closest to AgentDiet's "expired information" category and reads as a component, not a thesis.

### Cited Findings
- Landscape for thesis (i) (small-model gating of tool output): SWE-Pruner 0.6B skimmer with goal hint, −23–38% tokens, +1.2–1.4 pp solve on 500 Verified; SWE-Pruner Pro per-line keep/prune head, −39% tokens; Squeez 2B LoRA pruner with F1 0.80, no end-to-end eval; AgentDiet removes "useless, redundant, and expired" information, −39.9–59.7% input tokens at FSE 2026; Complexity Trap shows a zero-model masking baseline halves cost. — [SWE-Pruner](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.16746); [SWE-Pruner Pro](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18213); [Squeez](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.04979); [AgentDiet](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23586); [Complexity Trap](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.21433)
- Landscape for thesis (ii) (cache-aware compaction): survey of seven agents states standard compaction destroys the KV/prompt cache and that "no shipping agent is described as preserving the prompt cache through compaction"; Claude Code has "cross-session cache reuse"; blog literature names "cache-safe compaction" without describing it. — [compaction showdown](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/04/10/context-compaction-showdown-coding-agents/); [agentpatterns.ai](https://agentpatterns.ai/context-engineering/prompt-caching-architectural-discipline/)
- The only academic hit on cache reuse in agents is KVCOMM (multi-agent KV-cache sharing with context-aware offsets), not compaction. — [arXiv 2510.12872](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.12872)
- The Sept-2026 "When Can Agents Forget" paper reports cache-read token reductions (−33.3%) alongside input/output tokens, showing reviewers are starting to see cache-token accounting. — [arXiv 2609.29875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29875)
- Complexity Trap's own limitations list: "Observation Masking ignores observation relevance or staleness"; both strategies use "simple, non-adaptive heuristic triggers"; window size needed retuning per scaffold. — [arXiv HTML v3](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.21433v3)
- Landscape for thesis (iii) (recoverable elision): OpenHands keeps an append-only event log with suppression markers (full replay); Roo Code tags rather than deletes; Codex/Claude Code/Gemini discard originals; Pichay detects "faults" when the model re-requests evicted material and reports 0.0254% fault rate over 1.4M simulated evictions. — [compaction showdown](https://codex.danielvaughan.com/2026/04/10/context-compaction-showdown-coding-agents/); [Pichay](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.09023); [OpenHands condenser docs](https://docs.openhands.dev/sdk/arch/condenser)
- Cost-of-Pass (ICLR 2026) provides an accepted single-number metric (expected $ per correct answer) that folds accuracy and cost; HAL/"Agents That Matter" provide the Pareto framing. — [Cost-of-Pass](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.13359); [HAL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11977)
- "Don't Blame the LLM" establishes the methodology of fixing the model and varying only the harness across releases, with PR-level attribution. — [arXiv 2607.03691](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03691)

### Inferences (paper-shaped contributions and required experiments)
1. **"Break-even compaction: when summarizing costs more than it saves under prompt caching"** (thesis ii). Gap: clear. Claim shape: an analytical model of compaction cost (cache-write re-prefill + summarizer tokens + lost cache reads) vs expected savings over the remaining horizon, with a decision rule; then empirical validation. Cheapest convincing experiment: replay *existing* trajectories (e.g., SWE-bench Verified logs from SWE-agent/OpenHands, or the 60k-trajectory randomness dataset if released) through a cost simulator using provider-reported cache-read/write token counts and real price sheets (Anthropic, OpenAI, DeepSeek, Alibaba), comparing naive compaction, observation masking, tool-result clearing, and break-even gated compaction; then a small live confirmation (≥200 Verified instances, 2 models, 3 runs) where the metric is dollars at provider-reported usage. Because replay is deterministic, variance concerns are confined to the live confirmation. Strong SE/MLSys fit.
2. **"Lossless context: recoverable elision as a correctness property"** (thesis iii). Gap: mostly clear (Pichay is the nearest). Claim shape: define recoverability (byte-exact expand/recall), measure *recall rate* (how often the agent re-requests elided bytes) and *harm when recall is unavailable* (ablation: masked-but-unrecoverable vs recoverable). Cheapest experiment: same 200–500 Verified instances, three arms (raw, masked-unrecoverable à la Complexity Trap, masked-recoverable à la jevctx); report recall frequency, solve-rate non-inferiority CI, tokens, cost. This piggybacks on experiment 1's runs. Also measure the "fault" metric Pichay introduced to make results comparable.
3. **"Role-labelled admission gating vs summarization vs masking"** (thesis i). Gap: narrow; must beat SWE-Pruner (public code) and observation masking head-to-head on full 500 Verified with ≥2 models and 3 seeds, plus a judge-cost accounting (the judge's tokens count). The differentiator would be the *role* labels enabling retrieval later (link to thesis iii) and calibration from shadow logs. Most expensive and most crowded; recommend making it a section, not the thesis.
4. **Model-free supersession/staleness** (thesis iv): fold into 1–2 as the mechanism that makes break-even and recoverability cheap; compare against AgentDiet's "expired" category and Pichay's eviction as ablations.
- A combined framing that fits SE venues: "the harness, not the model: a cache- and recoverability-aware context manager," evaluated with the fixed-model/varied-harness methodology of arXiv 2607.03691, integrated as an OpenHands condenser or proxy so it is scaffold-agnostic (addresses Complexity Trap's "retune per scaffold" limitation).
- For harness influence, the artifact should be a proxy/condenser with a public price-sheet cost report and the "fault/recall rate" metric; release before submission so practitioners can cite it (as the OpenHands blog and Pichay did).

### Gaps
- No public dataset of SWE-bench trajectories with provider cache-read/write token counts was found; whether the 60k-trajectory dataset from arXiv 2602.07150 is released is unverified.
- Whether SWE-Pruner's released code runs out-of-the-box as a baseline was not checked.
